## Featured Project: Patient Readmission Prediction

The most significant project I have worked on so far is the Patient Readmission Prediction project. This work represents my first full step into the data pipeline, where I take raw information and turn it into something meaningful. The goal is to help hospitals understand which patients might need extra care after being discharged to prevent them from having to return within 30 days.

| Aspect | My Approach |
| --- | --- |
| **The Goal** | To understand and predict patient readmission using historical data. |
| **The Data** | Working with 3,000 patient records, focusing on age, stay duration, and health markers. |
| **The Tools** | Python, along with libraries like Pandas, NumPy, and Seaborn for data handling. |
| **The Methods** | Exploring different ways to "teach" the data to make predictions. |


## Learning Through Machine Learning

To better understand how to work with data, I have experimented with several different methods. I started with Decision Trees to see how simple logic can lead to a result, and moved on to Random Forest to see how combining many viewpoints can improve accuracy. I also explored K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) to understand how similar data points relate to one another, and XGBoost to see how powerful modern tools can be.

Through this project, I discovered that certain health markers, like blood sugar levels and blood pressure, along with the length of a hospital stay, are very important pieces of the puzzle. While I am still refining these models, this process has taught me a lot about the importance of balanced data and how to evaluate if a prediction is truly reliable.
